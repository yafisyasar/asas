# ✅ **UNIT 1 — INDIAN FINANCIAL SYSTEM**

### **Indian Financial System – Components**

A network of financial institutions, markets, instruments, services and regulations that mobilise savings and allocate funds.

**Components:**

1. **Financial Institutions** – Banks, NBFCs, LIC, Mutual Funds.
    
2. **Financial Markets** – Capital market, money market, forex market.
    
3. **Financial Instruments** – Shares, debentures, treasury bills, bonds.
    
4. **Financial Services** – Merchant banking, leasing, venture capital, mutual funds.
    

---

### **Role and Functions**

- Mobilising savings
    
- Facilitating investments
    
- Promoting economic development
    
- Providing liquidity
    
- Reducing risk by diversification
    
- Price discovery and capital formation
    

---

### **Interactions among Components**

Banks → provide loans  
Markets → provide trading platform  
Institutions → regulate  
Services → support fund raising  
Everything works together to channel funds from savers to users.

---

### **Recent Developments**

- Online trading
    
- Demat system
    
- SEBI reforms
    
- Growth of mutual funds
    
- New financial instruments
    
- Digital payments & fintech
    
- Introduction of derivatives
    

---

### **Financial Markets – Classification**

**A. Organised**  
– Capital market  
– Money market

**B. Unorganised**  
– Indigenous bankers, moneylenders

---

### **Capital Market**

Deals in long-term funds (>1 year).  
Includes: Primary market + Secondary market.

### **Money Market**

Deals in short-term funds (<1 year).  
Includes: Call money, treasury bills, commercial bills.

---

### **SEBI – Objectives**

- Protect investors
    
- Promote fair trading
    
- Regulate stock market
    
- Prevent malpractices
    

### **Functions**

- Regulates intermediaries
    
- Registers brokers, sub-brokers
    
- Issues guidelines for IPO, mutual funds
    
- Conducts inspections & audits
    
- Takes action against fraud
    

---

# ✅ **UNIT 2 — PRIMARY MARKET**

### **Primary Market**

Market for new securities issued for the **first time**.

### **Functions**

- Mobilise long-term capital
    
- Help companies raise funds
    
- Facilitate capital formation
    

---

### **Methods of New Issue**

1. **Public Issue** – IPO / FPO
    
2. **Right Issue** – Offered to existing shareholders
    
3. **Bonus Issue** – Free shares from reserves
    
4. **Private Placement** – Sold to selected investors
    

---

### **IPO**

Company issues shares to public for the first time.

### **FPO**

A listed company issues additional shares.

---

### **Public Issue**

Large-scale sale of shares to the public through prospectus.

### **Bonus Issue**

Free shares issued from profits/reserves to existing shareholders.

### **Right Issue**

Shares offered to existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings.

---

### **Intermediaries**

- Merchant bankers
    
- Registrars
    
- Underwriters
    
- Brokers
    
- Bankers to issue
    
- Advertisers
    

---

### **Innovative Financial Instruments**

- Derivatives
    
- Commercial Papers
    
- Zero coupon bonds
    
- Deep discount bonds
    
- Exchange traded funds
    
- Credit default swaps
    

---

#  **ESOP**
  
**Employees receive company shares at a discounted price as part of compensation. Helps motivate & retain employees.**

# ✅ **UNIT 3 — SECONDARY MARKET**

### **Secondary Market**

Market for second-hand / already issued securities.  
Example: Stock Exchanges.

---

### **Role & Functions of Stock Exchange**

- Offers liquidity & marketability
    
- Helps in price discovery
    
- Protects investor interests
    
- Encourages capital formation
    
- Provides trading platform
    

---

### **Stock Exchanges in India**

- BSE
    
- NSE
    
- OTCEI
    

---

### **Online Trading**

Buying and selling through electronic platform (NSE-NEAT, BSE-BOLT).  
Fast, transparent, and accessible globally.

---

### **Depositories**

A system where securities are held in **electronic (demat)** form.

**NSDL & CDSL** are major depositories.

---

### **Stock Market Indices**

Indicators showing market performance.  
Examples: NIFTY, SENSEX.

---

### **Types of Speculators**

1. **Bull** – Expects prices to rise
    
2. **Bear** – Expects prices to fall
    
3. **Stag** – Applies for new issues to sell at premium
    
4. **Lame Duck** – Speculator who cannot meet obligations
    

---

### **SEBI Regulations**

- Controls brokers & trading
    
- Insider trading rules
    
- Takeover code
    
- Listing norms
    
- Disclosure requirements
    

---

### **Foreign Institutional Investors**

Foreign entities investing in Indian securities (banks, funds, insurance firms).

### **Foreign Portfolio Investment**

Investment in Indian stocks, bonds by foreign investors for returns.

---

### **Private Equity**

Investment in unlisted companies for growth, restructuring or buyouts.

---

# ✅ **UNIT 4 — MUTUAL FUNDS**

### **Meaning**

A fund that pools money from many investors and invests in securities.

### **Objectives**

- Diversification of risk
    
- Professional fund management
    
- Liquidity
    
- Better returns
    

---

### **Advantages**

- Low risk
    
- Expert management
    
- Economies of scale
    
- Variety (debt, equity, balanced)
    
- Easy buying/selling
    

---

### **Classification**

1. **By Structure** – Open-ended, Close-ended
    
2. **By Objective** – Growth, Income, Balanced
    
3. **By Nature** – Equity, Debt, Hybrid
    
4. **ETFs** – Traded on stock exchange
    

---

### **ETF**

Exchange Traded Fund – behaves like a share but represents a basket of securities.

---

### **Constitution & Management**

Managed by AMC (Asset Management Company).  
Trustees oversee operations.  
Custodian holds securities.

---

### **Advantages & Limitations**

**Advantages:** Low cost, diversification, professional management.  
**Limitations:** Market risk, management fees, no guaranteed returns.

---

# ✅ **UNIT 5 — DERIVATIVES**

### **Derivatives**

Financial contracts whose value is derived from an underlying asset (shares, commodities, currency).

---

### **Features**

- No ownership of underlying
    
- Used for hedging & speculation
    
- High risk & high return
    
- Future dated contracts
    

---

### **Types of Derivatives**

1. **Forwards** – Private agreement to buy/sell later
    
2. **Futures** – Standardised forward traded on exchange
    
3. **Options** – Right but NOT obligation to buy/sell
    
4. **Swaps** – Exchange of cash flows (e.g., interest rate swap)
    

---

### **Forwards**

Customized OTC contract to buy/sell asset on a future date at a fixed price.

### **Futures**

Exchange-traded forward contract with margin, clearing house guarantee.

### **Options**

- **Call Option** – Right to buy
    
- **Put Option** – Right to sell
    

### **Swaps**

Agreement between two parties to exchange interest or currency payments.
